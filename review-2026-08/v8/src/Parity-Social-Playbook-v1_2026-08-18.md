# Parity Lending Social Playbook v1
### Targeting, posting, Stories, Reels and spend for the v8 master system
*Prepared 2026-08-18 for Rob Schrader. Companion to `DSCR-Social-Media-Strategy-Playbook.md` (2026-07-28), which holds the platform research and the compliance analysis. This document is the operating plan for the Parity brand specifically: what gets posted, where, how often, in what shape, who it targets, what it costs, and how it gets read.*

Sections 3 to 5 are the posting playbooks for feed, Stories and Reels. Section 6 is targeting, and it is mostly a list of what you cannot do and what to do instead. Section 7 is spend. Decisions already made and locked in this document, per Rob 2026-08-18: paid budget $300 to $500 a month for the first 60 days, boosts only, no cold prospecting. Paid delivery concentrated in the six pilot states (Ohio, South Carolina, Georgia, Texas, Florida, North Carolina). Video is text-motion generated from the v8 master, no voice, no face. Every paid click lands on paritylending.com/dscr, never a Meta instant form. Standing rules carried in from earlier calls: no rates, no timing claims, no Ameritrust or NMLS references, no dba line, no Equal Housing language, every CTA links to /dscr and never to Messenger, blue appears only in the logo mark.

## 1. The one-paragraph version

Parity has one master creative (v8), six messages, and two hero states. Every social slot on Facebook and Instagram is a re-cut of that master, so producing a week of content is a script run, not a design session. Post to the feed three times a week, Reels twice, Stories most days in short frame sets, and one carousel a week, on both platforms, using the same message that week so the test stays clean. Under Meta's financial products rules you cannot target investors, so creative is the targeting; paid dollars in the first 60 days go only to boosting organic posts that already showed a pulse, aimed at building the video-view and engagement audiences that are the only legal retargeting pool. Read tests on hook rate and click-through, not on cost per lead, and only act on big differences. None of it matters unless a human answers the 888 line inside five minutes, so the routing and voicemail fixes in Section 9 come before the first boost.

## 2. The repurposing system

The v8 master is a 4:5 (1080x1350) layout with fixed zones: header lockup, hero band with the angled panel and the DSCR HOME INVESTOR LOANS stack, spec strip, CTA bar, fine print. Every other slot is a deterministic re-cut of those zones. Nothing is redesigned; the generator gets a second output mode per slot.

| Slot | Size | What changes from the master | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feed portrait (master) | 1080x1350 | Nothing. This is the canonical unit. | FB feed, IG feed, boosted post |
| Feed square | 1080x1080 | Hero band shortened, spec strip collapses to one line of four items, CTA bar stays | IG grid consistency, FB feed |
| Story / Reel end card | 1080x1920 | Hero band taller, type block centred, spec strip vertical, CTA bar at bottom third with a large URL, safe zones respected (top 250px, bottom 340px clear) | IG and FB Stories, last 3 seconds of every Reel |
| Reel (text-motion) | 1080x1920, 15 to 20s | The story cut animated: photo pan, eyebrow fades in, DSCR stack builds line by line, red message lines type on, four spec ticks land in sequence, CTA end card holds 3s. Music from Meta's licensed library. No voice. | IG Reels, FB Reels |
| Carousel | 5 to 7 cards, 1080x1350 | Card 1 = master hero only (no spec strip). Cards 2 to 5 = one spec item per card at large scale with a one-line explanation. Last card = CTA bar full height. | IG and FB carousels, the highest-save format |
| Story frame set | 3 to 5 x 1080x1920 | Frame 1 hook (eyebrow + message lines), frame 2 the DSCR stack, frame 3 spec strip, frame 4 poll or question sticker, frame 5 CTA with link sticker | Daily Stories |
| Link preview | 1200x630 | Master hero cropped landscape, DSCR stack left, photo right | paritylending.com/dscr Open Graph image, so shared links carry the same look |
| Cover | 1640x856 | Flat state B, wide, mark left, spec strip right | FB Page cover, IG highlight covers use the ring icons |

Production rule: the week's message is fixed first, then the generator produces the whole slot set for that message and state in one run. Captions come from `parity-fb-v7-captions.md`, one caption per message, already stripped of the dba line and ending in the plain URL. That file needs no changes for v8.

Why carousels get a slot even though the master is a single image: Metricool's 2026 Instagram study (24.3M posts, 375K accounts, Jan to Feb 2026 vs 2025) has carousels producing nine times the saves of single images and outperforming them on every major metric, while single-image reach fell 22% and engagement 46% year over year. Reels produce four times the interactions of single images and are the only format that reliably reaches non-followers. Single images still matter as the boosted unit and for the grid, but they are no longer the growth format, so the calendar below leans on Reels and carousels for reach and uses the master image where it does its best work: paid.

## 3. Feed posting playbook

### 3.1 Cadence and calendar

Both platforms run the same message in the same week. Cross-posting is fine and encouraged; Facebook reach was the one major platform still growing in the 2026 data (+51% per the July playbook's Metricool cite), and the Parity Page has one follower, so there is nothing to protect by staggering.

| Day | Instagram | Facebook |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Feed post: master image (state A photo) | Same post |
| Tuesday | Stories: 4-frame set on the week's message | Stories: same frames |
| Wednesday | Reel: text-motion for the week's message | Same Reel |
| Thursday | Carousel: 5 to 6 cards on the week's message | Same carousel |
| Friday | Feed post: master image (state B flat) | Same post |
| Saturday | Reel: second cut (different photo or flat state) | Same Reel |
| Sunday | Stories: 3-frame recap with poll | Stories: same |

That is 3 feed images, 2 Reels, 1 carousel and 2 Story sets per week per platform. It is above what one person can sustain by hand and exactly what a generator plus a scheduler can sustain indefinitely, which is the point of the system.

### 3.2 The first six weeks

One message per week, in the order the calls prioritised them. Week 1 starts the Monday after Michael's verdicts land.

| Week | Message | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | M4 You're a borrower, not a lead | Michael's concept winner; the differentiator; sets the brand's tone |
| 2 | M1 No tax returns, the property qualifies | The clearest product statement; the control for every later read |
| 3 | M6 Hard money was the bridge | The highest-intent audience; ties to the takeout thesis and the lender-partner track |
| 4 | M3 The rehab is done | BRRRR investors, the defended niche from the July playbook |
| 5 | M2 Qualified on the rent | Spec-forward; tests whether specs or story pull harder |
| 6 | M5 Built for the serious investor | Portfolio scalers; the least differentiated line, so it goes last |

Weeks 7 to 12 repeat the cycle with state swapped (A becomes B) so each message gets both hero states over the twelve weeks. Nothing new is designed inside this window.

### 3.3 Caption rules

Caption per message from `parity-fb-v7-captions.md`. First line carries the hook, since Instagram truncates after roughly 125 characters. Last line is always the plain URL `paritylending.com/dscr`, because images are not clickable organically and Instagram is still withholding the bio link on the new account. Zero to three hashtags at most, and none in the first line: Metricool 2026 has posts with hashtags getting 32% fewer views and 34% fewer interactions than average, so hashtags are not a reach lever any more and mostly signal spam. If used, keep them to `#dscrloans #realestateinvesting #rentalproperty` in a first comment, not the caption. No rates, no timing, no "pre-approved", no consumer mortgage language, business-purpose line stays in every caption.

### 3.4 Publishing mechanics

Schedule through GHL Social Planner for now (the six deleted v6 drafts get replaced by the v8 week-1 set once Michael approves), because it posts to both the Page and @paritylend from one screen and logs to the same account. When volume justifies it, the Instagram Graph API publishes images, carousels and Reels directly and needs no browser; the account is already Business, portfolio-owned and Page-linked, which are the three prerequisites. Expect Meta to throttle publishing on a brand-new Instagram for the first days; if a scheduled post fails with a rate message, that is why, not a bug.

## 4. Stories playbook

Stories are the direct-connection format, and the one where a tiny account can look active without needing reach. Story replies rose 88% year over year in the Metricool data, and every reply lands as a DM in the GHL inbox once Instagram is connected there.

Frame set for a message week (all 1080x1920, generated from the master):

1. Hook frame: eyebrow and the two red message lines on white with the angled panel, no photo. Two seconds of reading.
2. Product frame: DSCR HOME INVESTOR LOANS stack on the flat navy hero with the colour mark.
3. Spec frame: the four ring icons stacked vertically with their labels, one line under each.
4. Engagement frame: a poll or question sticker over the flat hero. Polls: "Do you file taxes on your rentals through an LLC? Yes / No." "Ever had a bank say no to a rental purchase? Yes / No." Question sticker: "What's your DSCR? Drop the rent and the payment." The answers are content for future frames and a soft qualifier.
5. CTA frame: end card with the link sticker pointing to /dscr and the URL printed large under it as a fallback.

Post the set Tuesday, a 3-frame recap Sunday (hook, spec, CTA with poll). Never more than five frames in a set; completion drops fast after that. Pin the best sets to Highlights with ring-icon covers: How it works, Programs, Investors, FAQ. Highlights are the closest thing a new account has to a website.

Link sticker: available on Business accounts regardless of follower count and separate from the bio link, so use it from day one. If it is missing on the first attempt, that is the same new-account hold as the bio link and it clears on its own.

## 5. Reels playbook

Reels are the only format that reliably reaches non-followers, so they carry the growth job. Average watch time doubled to 8.5 seconds in the 2026 data, which is the whole budget: the hook lands by second two or the Reel is over.

Spec for the text-motion cut (no voice, no face, no AI voice, so nothing trips the platform inauthentic-content policies flagged in the July playbook):

- Length 15 to 20 seconds. Vertical 1080x1920. Safe zones: nothing that matters in the top 250px or bottom 340px.
- 0 to 2s: photo pans slowly, eyebrow snaps in, first red message line types on. This is the hook and it must be the strongest line of the message, not the product name.
- 2 to 6s: DSCR HOME INVESTOR LOANS builds line by line in navy on the angled panel.
- 6 to 12s: four spec ticks land in sequence with a soft click. Rent covers the payment. 20%+ down. 620+ credit. No tax returns.
- 12 to 17s: CTA end card holds. "See if you qualify in six quick questions. No credit pull." URL large. Red pill.
- Music: pick one bed from Meta's licensed business library and use it on every Reel for six weeks. Consistency compounds; switching tracks resets the test.
- On-screen text does the talking. Sparse, large, never a subtitle track. Nothing that reads as captions of a voice that is not there.
- Cover frame: the master image for that message, so the grid stays uniform.

Six scripts, one per message, are just the message lines above in the sequence given; the generator holds them. Second Reel of the week (Saturday) is the same script on the other photo or the flat state, which is the photo test in motion.

Post Reels to both platforms. Facebook Reels get their own distribution and the Page needs the inventory.

## 6. Targeting under Meta's rules

Every paid Parity ad, including a boosted post, must be declared under the Financial products and services special ad category. That declaration removes most of the targeting toolkit. The July playbook has the full table; the parts that decide this plan:

You cannot narrow age or gender, cannot use ZIP codes or exclude locations, cannot use detailed interest targeting for investors, cannot use lookalikes, and cannot rely on custom audiences as hard constraints because Advantage+ Audience is force-enabled on these campaigns and treats them as suggestions. Geographic targeting is allowed by state and by radius of at least 15 miles, which is exactly what the pilot-state decision uses.

What that means in practice: the creative is the targeting. A Reel that says "No tax returns. The property qualifies." self-selects investors out of a general audience, and Meta's delivery learns from who stops on it. Everything in Sections 3 to 5 is therefore also the targeting plan.

Set up now, before the first boost:

1. Ad account inside the Parity Lending Business Portfolio (id 28064934889861631), with the Page and @paritylend as assets. The Instagram account is already there and owned by the portfolio.
2. Meta Pixel installed on paritylending.com. The site is served by the Cloudflare Worker `parity-lending`, and the repo is the source of truth, so the pixel snippet goes into all five pages plus /dscr in the repo and then a Worker redeploy. Events: PageView everywhere, ViewContent on /dscr, Lead fired by the /dscr wizard on submit. The Conversions API is a one-click setup from Events Manager as of April 2026 and should be turned on the same day.
3. Audiences created in Ads Manager on day one so they start filling: Instagram engagers 365 days, Page engagers 365 days, video viewers 25% and 75% for all Reels, website visitors 180 days, /dscr visitors 30 days. These are the only legal retargeting pool. They are worthless empty, which is why boosting starts before lead-gen.
4. Geo set: Ohio, South Carolina, Georgia, Texas, Florida, North Carolina, as states, no exclusions.
5. Verification watch: Meta expanded financial-services advertiser verification during 2026 and its policy text says advertisers must show authorization "where this is a requirement." Parity is an unlicensed lead-generation brand and its loans are business-purpose. Do not assume a prompt will not appear. If Ads Manager asks for verification on the first boost, stop and bring it here before answering anything; the honest path is business verification of RSS Financial Holdings LLC, not any Ameritrust credential.

What not to spend time on: interest stacks, lookalikes, ZIP-level maps, TikTok ads, LinkedIn ads. The July playbook retires all of them for this product and nothing has changed.

## 7. Spend plan: $300 to $500 a month, boosts only

The first 60 days buy audiences and data, not leads. That is a deliberate choice, and the reason is arithmetic from the July playbook: at realistic DSCR CPLs a powered creative test costs tens of thousands, and at $500 a month a lead-gen campaign never leaves learning phase.

Weeks 1 and 2: zero paid. Post the calendar, connect the pixel, create the audiences, fix the phone routing. Read organic hook rate and CTR on every post after 48 hours.

Weeks 3 to 8: boost the winners only. A post qualifies for a boost when, at 48 hours, its organic hook rate (Reels) or click-through (feed) is in the top third of everything posted so far. Boost at $10 to $15 a day for 5 to 7 days. Objective for Reels is video views, because the job is filling the video-view audience. Objective for feed images and carousels is traffic to /dscr with the URL as the destination. Never more than two boosts live at once, so at $12 a day that is roughly $360 to $500 a month, which is the tier.

What to read: three-second hook rate and thruplay on Reels, link CTR on images, cost per landing-page view, /dscr wizard starts and completions from GHL, and the audience sizes growing week over week. Cost per lead is a guardrail, not a metric, at this budget; if a boosted post ever produces leads above roughly $150 each, note it and do not extrapolate.

Kill rules: stop a boost early if CTR is under half the account average after $30 spent, or if the comments are the wrong audience (personal home buyers asking about rates), which is a creative signal to reword the eyebrow.

The 60-day gate to Tier 2 ($1,000 to $2,500 on one Meta campaign with the v8 set inside one ad set): a five-minute answer on the 888 line proven on real inbound for at least four weeks, video-view and engagement audiences each above roughly 1,000 people, and at least one message with a visibly higher CTR than the others. If any of the three is missing, stay at Tier 1 another month; the money is not the constraint, the follow-up is.

On the lead-selling math from the 08-15 call: a $10 CPL that sells at $60 to two or three buyers is not a Meta number for a DSCR lead in a special ad category. The July research puts the realistic band at $60 to $180 for a raw form lead, with no study-grade benchmark below that. Treat lead resale as upside on leads Parity cannot place, not as the plan's revenue base, until there are 90 days of real CPL data.

## 8. Test discipline

The one-master rule exists so tests can be read. The rule for the twelve weeks: one variable per comparison, everything else frozen.

| Test | Variable | Read on | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Message | M1 to M6, one per week | Hook rate, CTR, wizard starts per 1,000 reach | A message beating the field by 50%+ on two of three becomes the default for paid |
| Hero state | Photo A vs flat B, alternated by week per message | Same | Only act if one state wins by 50%+ across at least three messages |
| Reel photo | Wednesday vs Saturday cut | Hook rate, thruplay | Same threshold |
| CTA line (month 3) | "See if you qualify" vs one challenger | CTR, wizard starts | Same |
| Layout challenger (month 2 or 3) | v8 master vs one alternate carrying the winning message and state | CTR, wizard starts | Only if the master is flat; if the challenger wins it becomes the master |

Log every post in a `creative_log.md` in the Parity folder with date, platform, message, state, format, reach, hook rate or CTR, spend, wizard starts, and a one-line note. The always-on agent already keeps this shape of log for email; this is the social twin. Small numbers stay small; do not read a 20% difference on 400 impressions as anything.

## 9. Speed to lead, the part that decides the outcome

Everything above generates a phone call or a wizard submit. The July playbook's finding stands: contact odds are 100 times higher inside five minutes than at thirty, most converted leads take six attempts, and half of leads never get a second call. At Parity's scale this is not an optimisation, it is the margin.

Current state, from the 08-14 phone work: 888-435-0314 rings Rob's LeadConnector app in Panama with recording on and the announcement playing. Michael was removed from the routing for testing and has not been put back. There is no voicemail greeting uploaded and the incoming call timeout is set to no timeout. SMS is permanently dead on this brand, so there is no automated text-back; the wizard's confirmation email is the only automatic touch.

Before the first boost: put Michael back on the ring (simultaneous, since GHL cannot cascade), upload a voicemail greeting, set the timeout, and write down the six-touch cadence for a wizard lead: call inside five minutes, second call same day, email day one (already automatic), call day two, email day three, call day five, final email day seven. Log every attempt on the GHL contact so the pipeline stage means something.

Handoff, per the 08-15 call: Rob takes the call, gathers value, loan amount, credit, zip, property type, rent, taxes, insurance and HOA, and either prices in the TPO pricer or three-ways Michael. That intake script is still unwritten and belongs in this folder next.
